Rates · Inflation-Linked · Story I

Oil Jumps, Yet Short-Dated Treasuries Rally

An energy supply shock is inflationary on paper. On 22 October the front end of the curve read it as a growth shock instead.

From our rates desk

The News

O22 Oct. — Oil rose above $110 a barrel after a Houthi drone strike near Saudi Aramco’s Shaybah oilfield, according to The National, which said operations were unaffected. According to BigGo Finance, short-term Treasury yields fell by as much as 20 basis points intraday, and analysts it quoted said growth concerns and financial-market jitters outweighed inflation pressure from oil. Market recaps described a sell-off in equities led by technology. The Fed announces its decision on 28 October.

The Plumbing

1. Splitting a nominal yield

A Treasury and a TIPS of the same maturity differ only in what they pay. The gap in yield is the breakeven inflation rate:

BE = ynominal − yreal

Breakeven is not a pure forecast. It also contains an inflation risk premium and a liquidity premium, because TIPS are less liquid than nominals. Real yield, in turn, reflects the expected path of the policy rate net of inflation, plus a real term premium.

2. Two channels from oil

An oil shock hits both legs. Channel one: higher energy prices lift expected headline inflation, so breakevens rise. Channel two: if the market thinks the shock will damage growth, or that it will push risk assets down and drive investors into safe assets, the expected policy path and real yields fall. The nominal yield moves by the sum of the two, so its direction tells you which channel won:

Figures box · 5-year point (illustrative numbers)
BeforeAfter
Nominal4.00%3.80%
Real (TIPS)1.60%1.30%
Breakeven2.40%2.50%

Here nominals rally 20bp, yet breakevens widen 10bp, because real yields fell 30bp. A nominal-only view reads this as “inflation fear faded”. The decomposition says the opposite for inflation expectations.

3. Expressing it, and the index lag

A breakeven widener is long TIPS and short a nominal of matching maturity, sized so that each leg has the same DV01. It profits when the breakeven rises, whatever the level of yields does. With $40k DV01 per leg: TIPS leg +30bp × $40k = +$1.2m, nominal leg −20bp × $40k = −$0.8m, net +$0.4m = 10bp × $40k.

TIPS principal accrues with an index that lags CPI by roughly three months. The carry on a linker over the next few weeks is therefore set by CPI prints that already exist. An oil spike today feeds the accrual only as later months roll into the reference index, so linker carry and breakeven repricing are different things.

Why It Matters for a Fund Analyst

  • Ask a rates manager to split the P&L: how much came from real yields and how much from breakevens?
  • Ask what an “inflation hedge” in a multi-asset fund really is. A TIPS fund has large real-rate duration and can fall when real yields rise even if inflation does too.
  • Check how the fund reports duration: nominal DV01, real DV01 and breakeven DV01 are separate exposures.

Concepts: FI-INFLATION · FI-CARRY-ROLL · FI-DURATION

  2. The market priced mainly a growth hit: the expected policy path and real yields fell by more than breakevens rose.

Answers — tap here, or turn the page · Story I

1. C — 2.40%.

BE = 4.00% − 1.60% = 2.40%.

A adds the two yields. B divides 4.00 by 1.60, a ratio instead of a spread. D is the real yield itself.

2. A — about +$400k.

Long TIPS gains when real yields fall: 30 × $40k = +$1.2m. Short Treasuries lose when yields fall: 20 × $40k = −$0.8m. Net +$0.4m, which is the 10bp breakeven widening times $40k.

B adds the absolute moves (50bp). C has the sign wrong. D counts only the TIPS leg.

3. B — a growth hit dominated.

The nominal yield is real yield plus breakeven. For it to fall while an inflationary shock hits, the fall in real yields and the expected policy path must exceed any rise in breakevens.

A confuses the nominal move with the breakeven move. C infers a term-premium collapse from oil, which does not follow. D mixes up the accrual index with market pricing: yields reprice at once.

Private Credit · Fund Structures · Story II

A 5% Cap Meets a 39% Request

Non-traded BDCs sell a quarterly exit. The tender mechanics decide how long that exit actually takes.

From our credit desk

The News

TFilings — According to an SEC Form 8-K, Blue Owl Technology Income Corp. received estimated repurchase requests of 39.0% of its shares outstanding in the third quarter of 2026. It said it would fulfil its tender offer of 5%, with pro-rata fills. Separately, an SEC tender-offer filing from Onex Direct Lending BDC Fund says the fund authorised a cash tender for up to 5% of its outstanding shares, expiring at 11:59 p.m. New York time on 6 November 2026 unless extended.

The Plumbing

1. A tender is not a redemption right

A non-traded BDC does not owe holders their NAV on demand. The board chooses, each quarter, to make an issuer tender offer for a stated percentage of shares, commonly 5% of the total. If tendered shares exceed that amount, the fund buys the same fraction of each holder’s request:

fill = cap ÷ requests

illustrative: 5% ÷ 20% = 25% of each request is paid

This is a structural limit announced in advance. It is different from a suspension, where the fund stops paying altogether.

2. The queue

An investor who wants out in full, and resubmits the unfilled part each quarter, gets paid a fraction of what is left. If the fill ratio is f every quarter, the share still invested after n quarters is:

remaining = (1 − f)n

Figures box · exit queue (illustrative numbers)
Requests / capFillLeft after 4 qtrs
10% / 5%50%6%
20% / 5%25%32%
39% / 5%12.8%58%

Assumes requests stay at the same level each quarter and the investor resubmits everything.

There is a feedback loop. An investor who expects a 25% fill and wants $10m must ask for $40m. Rational over-requesting inflates the request ratio, which cuts the fill ratio, which prompts more over-requesting.

3. Where the cash comes from

The fund funds a tender from loan repayments, cash, liquid assets and its credit facility. Each tender shrinks equity, so leverage and the share of illiquid assets rise for those who stay unless new subscriptions arrive. Loan marks are Level 3 and set by the manager’s valuation process, so the NAV used for the tender can lag where the loans would trade. That makes the exit price itself a question for diligence.

Why It Matters for a Fund Analyst

  • Ask for fill ratios and request ratios by quarter, and for the number of investors who resubmitted.
  • Ask for sources of liquidity against a full 5% tender: quarterly repayments, liquid sleeve, undrawn facility.
  • Ask whether the board has ever tendered above or below the usual cap, and what rule it follows.

Concepts: AL-GATES · AL-EVERGREEN · DD-LIQ-MISMATCH

Market Structure · Repo · Story III

Treasury Repo Clearing: Netting, Margin and SOFR

The mandate moves bilateral repo into a clearing house. The benchmark may barely change. Dealer balance sheets and borrowers’ margin bills will.

From our market-structure desk

The News

TBackground — The Office of Financial Research has published an analysis, “How the Treasury Clearing Rule for Repo Might Affect SOFR”, which, according to coverage of the post, finds higher volume but minimal effect on the level of SOFR. Crane Data’s summary says the OFR built a hypothetical SOFR that includes bilateral repo, and that 42% of the sampled 2022 pilot volume would have been cleared under the rule. As of the SEC’s August 2026 update, as reported, the compliance date for eligible repo is 30 June 2027. SOFR was 3.88% on 21 October, according to NY Fed data on FRED.

The Plumbing

1. Netting is the prize

A dealer borrows cash from money funds and lends it to hedge funds against Treasuries. Bilaterally, each trade sits on its balance sheet gross. At a central counterparty, both trades face the same counterparty, so where accounting conditions are met (same maturity, same master netting terms) offsetting trades can be netted. Gross assets fall, which lowers the leverage-ratio denominator.

Figures box · dealer netting (illustrative numbers)
BilateralCleared
Repo + reverse$150bn$10bn
Total exposure$900bn$760bn
Tier 1 $30bn: SLR3.33%3.95%

Exposure falls by gross $150bn less the net $10bn left, or $140bn. SLR = 30 ÷ 760 = 3.95%. This is the “balance sheet space” argument for clearing. It lets dealers intermediate more Treasury financing for the same capital.

2. Margin is the cost

A clearing house collects initial margin (IM) plus daily variation margin. Some bilateral trades are done at a zero haircut, so moving them into clearing creates a new funding need:

max position = equity ÷ IM rate

illustrative: $2bn ÷ 2% = $100bn of Treasuries

A leveraged relative-value fund must hold margin against every dollar of repo, so its maximum leverage is capped at 1 ÷ IM rate (50× at 2%). Whether margin is posted directly or through a sponsoring dealer decides who bears the balance-sheet cost.

3. Why SOFR need not move

SOFR is a volume-weighted median of overnight Treasury repo rates. Adding trades that were previously uncounted changes its composition. If the new trades are priced like those already in, the median barely shifts, even though the quantities of balance sheet and margin underneath it change a lot. The rule dates are regulatory claims as of the sources above and should be spot-checked.

Why It Matters for a Fund Analyst

  • Ask a basis-trade manager about margin at the CCP: IM rate, who posts it, and the stressed IM add-on.
  • Ask how financing terms change: does the fund keep access at zero haircut, or is it moving to sponsored clearing?
  • Ask about dealer capacity in stress: netting helps in calm markets, but IM rises when volatility does.

Concepts: MS-TREASURY-CLEARING · FI-REPO · CB-SLR

  1. SOFR will fall by the amount of initial margin collected.
  2. Clearing removes all counterparty risk, so repo spreads vanish.
  3. The rule matters only for money market funds, not for dealers.

Answers — tap here, or turn the page · Story III

7. C — 3.95%.

Gross removed = 80 + 70 = 150, but 10 stays on as the net position, so exposure falls by 140 to 760. 30 ÷ 760 = 3.947%.

A ignores netting (30 ÷ 900). B removes only the smaller leg (30 ÷ 830). D removes all $150bn and forgets the residual $10bn (30 ÷ 750).

8. A — $100bn.

Position × 2% ≤ $2bn, so position ≤ $2bn ÷ 0.02 = $100bn, which is 50× leverage.

B multiplies equity by the margin rate instead of dividing. C divides by 0.98, treating the haircut as a financing share. D slips a decimal (÷ 20%).

9. D — capacity and leverage change even if SOFR does not.

A benchmark’s median can be stable while the volume of netting and the margin held change. Both feed through to dealer intermediation and to borrowers’ maximum leverage.

A confuses collateral with the price of cash. B overstates what a CCP does: it mutualises and manages risk, it does not remove it. C: dealers are the main beneficiaries of netting.
