Rates · Government Bonds · Story I · New concept

The Biggest Yield Drop Since 2023, Priced Properly

The 10-year fell back below 5% as investors ran from AI-linked equities. A one-day move this size is a duration question first, and a correlation question second.

From our rates desk

The News

T22 Oct. — The 10-year Treasury yield fell to about 4.95% on Thursday, its largest one-day drop since March 2023, according to reports from 24/7 Wall St. and Seoul Economic Daily. The move came as investors sold AI-linked technology stocks following Meta’s results, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.6%. CNBC’s headline put it as the yield dropping “back to 5%” as investors sought cover from AI worries. Analysts quoted by Seoul Economic Daily said the drop did not mean the anxieties weighing on markets had eased. Traders were watching the Fed meeting on 27–28 October and PCE inflation data on 29 October.

The Plumbing

1. What a big yield move does to a bond

For a bond, the price response to a yield change Δy has two terms. The first is duration, which is linear. The second is convexity, which is quadratic and always helps the holder of a plain bond:

ΔP / P ≈ −Dmod × Δy

   + ½ × C × (Δy)²

Because the convexity term is the same whichever way yields move, gains from a fall exceed losses from an equal rise. The effect is small for a one-day move of this kind and material for a 100bp move, as the box shows.

2. Why yields fell: the stock–bond correlation

Yields fell here because the shock was about growth and equity risk appetite, not about inflation. In that regime Treasuries rally when stocks fall, so the correlation between bond and equity returns is negative and bonds hedge. If the dominant shock is inflation or term premium, as in 2022, bonds and stocks fall together and the correlation turns positive. The sign is a feature of the regime, not a constant.

σp² = we²σe² + wb²σb²

The same weights and volatilities give a portfolio about 1.6 points riskier when the correlation flips from −0.3 to +0.3. A fund that uses duration as its equity hedge is making a regime bet.

Why It Matters for a Fund Analyst

  • Ask how the hedge was tested. Does the back-test include inflation-shock periods, or only the post-2000 regime of negative correlation?
  • Ask for convexity alongside duration for any fund that uses long bonds as a tail hedge, since the payoff in a rally is what you are buying.
  • Separate level from path. A one-day fall says nothing about whether the term premium has changed, and a Fed meeting is days away.

Duration: 7.8 × 1% = 7.80%. Convexity: ½ × 70 × (0.01)² = 0.35%. Sum: 8.15%.

Credit · Derivatives · Story II

A CDS Quote Above 1,000bp Is a Price, Not a Verdict

CoreWeave’s credit default swaps trade at levels analysts call distressed. Reading the quote correctly takes three conversions.

From our credit desk

The News

C23 Oct. — CNBC reported CoreWeave’s five-year credit default swap spread at about 1,145 basis points on 21 October and about 1,129 on 23 October. An analyst at S&P Global Market Intelligence told CNBC that roughly 1,000bp or more generally signals credit distress. CNBC and Benzinga put the yield on CoreWeave’s 9.75% 2031 bond at roughly 15.6–15.8%. CNBC reported total debt of about $51.6bn and a share price down about 36% in October. Blackstone’s Jon Gray said the company can work through its financing pressure.

The Plumbing

1. From spread to default probability

A CDS spread pays for protection against loss. With a constant hazard rate λ and recovery R, the spread approximates expected loss per year:

s ≈ λ × (1 − R)

PDT = 1 − e−λT

Illustrative numbers · 10% spread, 5 years
 λ5-yr PD
R = 40%16.7%56.5%
R = 20%12.5%46.5%

Notice the direction: for a given spread, a lower assumed recovery means a lower implied default probability, because each default costs more. The number depends on the recovery convention.

2. Risk-neutral, not real-world

That probability is risk-neutral. It contains the risk premium that sellers of protection require, hedging demand from lenders and holders who cannot sell their debt, and liquidity effects in a thin market. For a name with heavy hedging demand the real-world default probability is typically lower than the implied one. The quote is a price of protection, not a forecast.

3. Upfront, and the basis

When the spread is far above the standard coupon (5% for the standard high-yield-style contract), the buyer pays the difference up front:

Upfront ≈ (s − coupon) × RPV01

illustrative: (10% − 5%) × 3.1 ≈ 15.5 points

The CDS–bond basis compares the two prices of the same risk: basis = CDS spread − bond spread. A negative basis means the bond looks cheap against its protection. Funding costs, bond illiquidity and the cheapest-to-deliver option all feed it, so a large basis is not free money.

Why It Matters for a Fund Analyst

  • Use CDS as a cross-check on marks. If a private-credit fund holds loans to AI-infrastructure borrowers, ask how its Level 3 marks compare with the traded CDS and bond spreads of comparable names.
  • Ask for look-through exposure to data-centre and GPU-financed borrowers across direct lending, ABS and CLO collateral.
  • Ask about recovery assumptions. Collateral that depreciates quickly, such as hardware, deserves a lower recovery than a typical senior secured loan.

Concepts: DV-CDS · CR-SPREADS · CR-DEFAULT-LGD · CR-MARKLAG

Answers — tap here, or turn the page · Story II

4. B — about 12.5%.

λ = 0.08 ÷ 0.60 = 13.33%. PD = 1 − e−0.1333 = 12.5%.

A treats the spread as the probability and ignores recovery. C multiplies by (1 − R) instead of dividing. D is the hazard rate, not the probability over the year.

5. B — risk-neutral and recovery-dependent.

With R = 40%, 1,100bp gives λ = 18.3% and a five-year PD of 60.0%, so the arithmetic is right. The objection is what the number means: it is a risk-neutral price of protection.

A misreads a spread as a probability. C is too absolute; the conversion works, with caveats. D is wrong because upfront quotes convert back to a running spread.

6. B — 15.5 points.

(10% − 5%) × 3.1 = 15.5 points, paid by the buyer because the spread exceeds the coupon.

A forgets the annuity. C applies the annuity to the full spread instead of the excess over the coupon. D is a sign error: the seller pays upfront only when the spread is below the coupon.

Equities · Index Construction · Story III

The Nasdaq Falls 2.6% While the Equal-Weight Index Rises

The same session gave two different answers to “what did the market do?” The difference is weights, and it matters for risk measurement.

From our equities desk

The News

T22 Oct. — The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.6% to 26,008.86, its worst session since early June, according to Kiplinger and Mitrade. The cap-weighted S&P 500 fell about 1.2%, while the equal-weight S&P 500 rose, by between roughly 0.3% and 0.8% depending on the outlet (Mitrade, Schwab, Chartmill). Meta fell roughly 7% after guiding 2026 capital spending to $135–145bn. Money moved into defensive sectors.

The Plumbing

1. Weights decide the headline

A cap-weighted index return is R = Σ wiri, with weights proportional to market value. An equal-weight index uses 1/N for every stock. The gap is:

Rcap − Req = Σ (wi − 1/N) ri

It is positive when large stocks outperform and negative when they fall behind. A single theme concentrated in the largest names, here AI capital spending, can move the headline without moving the median stock.

Illustrative numbers · 500 stocks
 WeightReturn
Top 1035%−4.0%
Other 49065%+0.6%
Cap-weighted −1.0%
Equal-weighted +0.5%

2. Measuring the concentration

The effective number of stocks is the inverse of the sum of squared weights. It falls as weight piles into a few names:

Neff = 1 ÷ Σ wi²

An equal-weight portfolio of 500 stocks has Neff = 500. A cap-weighted index with the same 500 names has a much lower figure, so it behaves like a smaller portfolio.

3. Tracking error

Tracking error is the volatility of active return. It scales with the square root of time:

TEannual = TEdaily × √252

illustrative: 0.40% × 15.87 ≈ 6.3%

An equal-weight fund measured against a cap-weighted benchmark can show tracking error of this size even though it holds the same stocks.

Why It Matters for a Fund Analyst

  • Check which benchmark the fund reports against. Beating the equal-weight index and lagging the cap-weighted one tell different stories.
  • Ask for the effective number of holdings and the top-10 weight, not just the count of positions.
  • Check factor overlap. If several managers in the line-up hold the same mega-cap theme, diversification across them is smaller than it looks.

Concepts: EQ-INDEX · RP-TRACKING-ERROR · EQ-FACTOR

10. A CLO has performing collateral par of $450m. The Class A and Class B notes total $360m. The Class A/B overcollateralisation test requires a ratio of at least 120%. By roughly what percentage can performing par fall before the test fails?

  1. 5.0%
  2. 4.0%
  3. 16.7%
  4. 20.0%
Answers — tap here, or turn the page · Back Page

10. B — 4.0%.

The ratio today is 450 ÷ 360 = 125%. The test fails when par falls below 1.20 × 360 = $432m. The cushion is 18 ÷ 450 = 4.0%. After a failure, cash is diverted from equity to pay down the senior notes.

A uses coverage points (125 − 120) as a percentage of par. C is Class B’s share of the rated notes (60 ÷ 360). D subtracts 100% from the trigger.
