What It Does

Following the 2023 spin-off of its consumer segment as Kenvue, Johnson & Johnson operates two pure-play divisions: Innovative Medicine (~55% of revenues) and MedTech. Innovative Medicine is led by Darzalex (multiple myeloma), Tremfya (psoriatic disease), Spravato (treatment-resistant depression), and Carvykti (CAR-T cell therapy). MedTech covers cardiovascular devices via the Shockwave acquisition (>90% gross margins), orthopaedics through DePuy Synthes, and a surgical robotics platform (Ottava, in late-stage development). The company has raised its dividend for 61 consecutive years — one of 35 S&P 500 Dividend Kings.

Edge vs. Peers

  • No single-franchise cliff risk. Pfizer faces a post-COVID revenue wall; AbbVie's Humira succession creates execution risk; Merck's pipeline is Keytruda-concentrated, with its own biosimilar clock starting post-2028. J&J's top-line is diversified across four therapeutic pillars — no single product exceeds ~22% of group revenue — meaning a setback in one area does not threaten the thesis.
  • Carvykti supply moat. CAR-T therapy requires bespoke cell manufacturing; competitors Bristol Myers Squibb and Pfizer have suffered repeated production failures. J&J now leads on delivered-dose volumes in relapsed/refractory myeloma — a structural supply advantage in a high-ASP market where there is no oral substitute and ~24,000 eligible US patients annually.
  • Two-division earnings resilience. The combination of IP-protected pharma margins and MedTech's recurring device-replacement cycles (patients returning for implant replacements on predictable timelines) produces structurally lower earnings volatility than any pure-play pharma peer. In the last three downturns, J&J's EPS declined less than half as much as PFE, ABBV, or MRK.
  • Capital-allocation credibility. The Kenvue spin-off unlocked $13.2bn for shareholders and sharpened R&D focus. Sixty-one consecutive years of dividend growth — maintained through nine recessions — signals a management culture that treats shareholder returns as a contract. No peer holds the same streak.

No option-exercise motive at this seniority; Clark has full visibility to the talc litigation reserve and bought anyway. (SEC EDGAR Form 4, 10 Oct 2026)

Why It Fits a Value Lens

  • Moat / quality: ROIC of 18.3% (5-yr avg) versus sector median ~12%. Darzalex–Tremfya–Carvykti IP protected through at least 2029–2031. Shockwave cardiovascular portfolio adds high-barrier recurring device revenue with >90% gross margins.
  • Price vs value: At 15.2× TTM P/E versus its own 5-year median of 17.8×, and an FCF yield of 6.4% versus the sector's 4.0%, J&J trades at a 15% discount to its historical valuation. The 22% MoS to analyst consensus ($199.50) is the widest since the COVID sell-off in 2020.

Key Risks

  • Talc litigation overhang. ~60,000 suits pending after federal appeals court rejected J&J's third bankruptcy pre-pack (early 2026). $8bn reserved but final exposure uncertain. An adverse bellwether verdict could trigger step-change in settlement expectations — this is the primary reason the stock trades at a discount and the most credible value-trap scenario.
  • Stelara biosimilar acceleration. If biosimilars take >25% share by end-2026 — plausible given Organon and Amgen pricing — FY2027 revenues could miss consensus by $1.5–2bn. The stock has partially re-rated since initial biosimilar entry; a second down-leg demands patience beyond a 12-month horizon.
  • Ottava delay risk. Surgical robot FDA submission slipped to late 2027. Any further slip removes the growth optionality some sell-side models embed in their $199+ price targets, compressing the intrinsic-value ceiling underpinning the MoS estimate.
